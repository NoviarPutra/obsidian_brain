# Daily Life & Practical Communication

## 1. Core Vocabulary & Idiomatic Expressions

1. **run errands** *(phrase)* `/rʌn ˈer.əndz/`
   - *Definition*: To make short trips to complete everyday tasks like grocery shopping, posting parcels, or visiting the bank.
   - *Example*: I need to run a few errands this afternoon before the supermarket closes.

2. **catch up with (someone)** *(phrasal verb)* `/kætʃ ʌp wɪð/`
   - *Definition*: To meet or talk with someone you have not seen recently to exchange personal news.
   - *Example*: Let us meet for coffee on Friday so we can finally catch up with each other.

3. **commute** *(noun / verb)* `/kəˈmjuːt/`
   - *Definition*: The daily journey to and from work or school, or to travel this distance regularly.
   - *Example*: My morning commute takes forty minutes because the local trains are usually delayed.

4. **put off** *(phrasal verb)* `/pʊt ɒf/`
   - *Definition*: To delay or postpone an event, meeting, or task until a later date or time.
   - *Example*: You should not put off cleaning your apartment until Sunday evening arrives.

5. **get used to (something / doing something)** *(phrase)* `/ɡet juːzd tuː/`
   - *Definition*: To become familiar with a new situation, routine, or environment through repeated experience.
   - *Example*: It took me several months to get used to waking up early for work.

6. **reliable** *(adjective)* `/rɪˈlaɪ.ə.bəl/`
   - *Definition*: Deserving trust; dependable and working consistently well without breaking down or failing.
   - *Example*: Public transportation in this city is very reliable, so few people drive cars.

## 2. Grammar & Sentence Pattern Focus

### Pattern 1: Present Perfect with *already*, *just*, and *yet*

- **Structure**:
  - Positive: `Subject + have / has + (already / just) + Past Participle`
  - Negative: `Subject + have / has + not + Past Participle + ... + yet`
  - Question: `Have / Has + Subject + Past Participle + ... + yet?`
- **Explanation**: Use this pattern to talk about recent actions, life events, or unfinished tasks connected to the present moment. *Just* shows an action happened moments ago; *already* shows completion sooner than expected; *yet* appears in questions and negatives to discuss expected events.
- **Examples**:
  1. I have **already** finished my weekly grocery shopping, so my refrigerator is full.
  2. David has **just** called to say that he is waiting at the bus stop.
  3. Have you replied to the landlord's message about the broken heating **yet**?

### Pattern 2: First Conditional with *if* and *unless*

- **Structure**:
  - `If + Present Simple, ... will / won't + Base Verb`
  - `Unless + Present Simple, ... will / won't + Base Verb` *(Note: "Unless" means "If not")*
- **Explanation**: Use the first conditional to express real, likely future possibilities and their direct consequences in daily routines or planning.
- **Examples**:
  1. If the train arrives on time, I will meet you outside the station at six.
  2. We will miss our doctor appointment unless we leave the house right now.
  3. If you do not write down your schedule, you will forget important daily tasks.

## 3. Reading Passage & Dialogue

**Title: Finding a Balance on a Busy Thursday**

*Setting: Elena and Mark are chatting during their lunch break at a city café.*

**Elena**: Have you finished organizing your schedule for the upcoming weekend yet?

**Mark**: Not completely. I have just sent an email to my repair technician, but he has not responded yet. If he does not fix my washing machine tomorrow, I will spend my entire Saturday running errands across town.

**Elena**: That sounds stressful. You always put off your household chores until the weekend, which ruins your free time. Why don't you do small tasks during your weekday evenings instead?

**Mark**: I usually return home late, and my daily commute takes almost an hour. However, I have already started preparing my meals in advance, so that saves me some time. I am slowly getting used to this new routine.

**Elena**: That is a good step. If you finish your laundry on Friday night, we can catch up with Sarah over brunch on Sunday. She has already booked a table at that new bakery near the central park.

**Mark**: That sounds like a wonderful plan. Unless my manager gives me extra work tomorrow, I will definitely join you both for brunch.

## 4. Comprehension & Usage Check

1. **Detail Check**: Why might Mark have to spend his Saturday running errands instead of relaxing?
2. **Vocabulary Application**: Fill in the blank using the correct form of *catch up with*, *put off*, or *commute*:
   > *"If you continue to ____________ paying your utility bills, you will receive a penalty notice."*
3. **Grammar Transformation**: Combine these two sentences into one using the First Conditional with **unless**:
   > *Sentence A: The weather must improve.*
   > *Sentence B: Otherwise, we will cancel our outdoor picnic.*

## 5. Discussion Prompts

1. Describe your typical weekday morning routine. What is one habit that makes your day run smoothly?
2. How do you usually travel during your daily commute, and how reliable is the transportation in your area?
3. What is a household chore or administrative errand that you often put off, and why do you delay it?
4. If you have an entirely free weekend with no obligations, who do you usually catch up with and what do you do?
