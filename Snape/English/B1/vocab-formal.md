# vocab-formal

## 1. Core Vocabulary & Idiomatic Expressions

* **Assist** /əˈsɪst/ (*verb*)
  * *Definition*: To help someone complete a task, solve a problem, or provide service.
  * *Example*: If you need support with the project, our team can assist you tomorrow afternoon.
* **Inform** /ɪnˈfɔːm/ (*verb*)
  * *Definition*: To officially tell someone important information or give notice about an event.
  * *Example*: We have already informed the manager that the report was delayed by technical issues.
* **Require** /rɪˈkwaɪə(r)/ (*verb*)
  * *Definition*: To need something specific or make it necessary according to rules.
  * *Example*: This job position will require basic computer skills and two years of relevant experience.
* **Inquire** /ɪnˈkwaɪə(r)/ (*verb*)
  * *Definition*: To ask for information in a polite, professional, or formal manner.
  * *Example*: I called the front office yesterday to inquire about the schedule for next week.
* **Receive** /rɪˈsiːv/ (*verb*)
  * *Definition*: To get, collect, or accept something that has been sent to you.
  * *Example*: Have you received the confirmation email that was sent by the customer service team?
* **Apologize** /əˈpɒl.ə.dʒaɪz/ (*verb*)
  * *Definition*: To express regret and say sorry for a mistake or unexpected inconvenience.
  * *Example*: The director wants to apologize for the delay because our servers were temporarily down.
* **Regarding** /rɪˈɡɑː.dɪŋ/ (*preposition*)
  * *Definition*: About, concerning, or with reference to a particular subject or problem.
  * *Example*: If you have any questions regarding the new policy, please contact human resources today.

## 2. Grammar & Sentence Pattern Focus

### Target 1: Passive Voice in Formal Communication
* **Structure**: `Subject + am/is/are/was/were + Past Participle (+ by agent)`
* **Explanation**: Formal English uses the passive voice to emphasize the action or outcome rather than the person performing it.
* **Examples**:
  1. The final budget was approved by the director before the meeting started yesterday.
  2. All important documents are stored in the secure database to protect user privacy.
  3. Your application was reviewed carefully before the hiring team made their final decision.

### Target 2: Relative Clauses with "Which" and "Who"
* **Structure**: `Main Clause + who/which + Defining Information`
* **Explanation**: Relative clauses combine related facts into one clear sentence, avoiding short, informal phrasing.
* **Examples**:
  1. We hired an experienced specialist who has resolved several major network problems this month.
  2. She forwarded the official email which contained the updated guidelines for our next project.
  3. The manager contacted the client who requested additional technical information about our software.

## 3. Reading Passage & Dialogue

**Context**: Elena and Marcus are preparing a formal status update email for a corporate client.

**Elena**: Good morning, Marcus. Have you received the feedback from our primary client yet?

**Marcus**: Yes, I have read their message regarding the delivery dates for the software.

**Elena**: Did they require any major adjustments to the timeline we prepared last week?

**Marcus**: Fortunately, they only asked us to clarify a few details about user training.

**Elena**: That is great news, but we should inform them about the server maintenance.

**Marcus**: I agree. If we send an update now, they will appreciate our clear communication.

**Elena**: Should we also apologize for the short delay in delivering the draft yesterday?

**Marcus**: Yes, because professional courtesy is valued by every organization we work with today.

**Elena**: Let's draft an email that includes all solutions clearly and outlines the steps.

**Marcus**: I will assist you with the draft after I finish this quick phone call.

**Elena**: Perfect. We can contact the manager who approved the original contract for confirmation.

**Marcus**: Excellent plan. If we finish this today, our client will be completely satisfied.

**Elena**: Please bring the file which contains the financial breakdown for the upcoming quarter.

**Marcus**: I have already printed the document so that we can review it together quickly.

**Elena**: If we follow this procedure, potential misunderstandings will be avoided very easily.

**Marcus**: Exactly, because clear communication ensures that all project goals are met on time.
