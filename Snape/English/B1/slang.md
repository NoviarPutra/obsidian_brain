# slang

## 1. Core Vocabulary & Idiomatic Expressions

- **hang out** /hæŋ aʊt/ *(phrasal verb)*: To spend time relaxing or socializing casually with other people.  
  *Example:* We usually hang out at the local coffee shop after finishing our afternoon classes.

- **chill** /tʃɪl/ *(adjective / verb)*: Relaxed and easygoing; or to relax and take things easy.  
  *Example:* My roommate is very chill, so our apartment is always quiet and peaceful.

- **grab a bite** /ɡræb ə baɪt/ *(idiom)*: To get something quick and informal to eat.  
  *Example:* If you are hungry, we can grab a bite before the movie starts tonight.

- **no big deal** /noʊ bɪɡ diːl/ *(phrase)*: Not important, not serious, or not worth worrying about.  
  *Example:* Don't worry about being late; it is really no big deal for us.

- **heads up** /ˈhedz ʌp/ *(noun phrase)*: An advance warning, reminder, or useful piece of information.  
  *Example:* Thanks for the heads up about the schedule change before I left my house.

- **catch up** /kætʃ ʌp/ *(phrasal verb)*: To talk to someone you have not seen recently to share news.  
  *Example:* I haven't seen Maria for months, so we need to catch up this weekend.

## 2. Grammar & Sentence Pattern Focus

### Target 1: Second Conditional (Hypothetical Situations)
- **Structure:** `If + Subject + Past Simple, Subject + would/could + Base Verb`
- **Explanation:** Use this structure to talk about imaginary, unreal, or unlikely situations in the present or future.
- **Examples:**
  1. If I had more free time today, I would hang out with my friends at the beach.
  2. If they lived closer to the city center, we could grab a bite together more often.
  3. What would you do if your best friend forgot to give you a heads up?

### Target 2: Defining and Non-Defining Relative Clauses (*who*, *which*, *that*)
- **Structure:** `Main Clause + [who / which / that + relative clause]`
- **Explanation:** Use relative pronouns to combine ideas and describe people, places, or things without starting a new sentence.
- **Examples:**
  1. I prefer to spend time with people who have a chill and positive attitude.
  2. The new cafe, which opened last Monday, is a great place to grab a bite.
  3. That is the casual restaurant that we visited when we wanted to catch up.

## 3. Reading Passage & Dialogue

**Leo:** Hey Maya, have you spoken to Sam recently? I haven't seen him around our campus for two weeks.

**Maya:** Yes, he called me yesterday evening! If he had more free time, he would hang out with us tonight.

**Leo:** That makes sense. His new job, which is very demanding, takes up almost all of his daily energy.

**Maya:** Exactly, but he gave me a heads up that he is completely free this coming Saturday afternoon.

**Leo:** That sounds fantastic. We should definitely meet near the downtown square and grab a quick bite to eat.

**Maya:** That works for me. I can message the other classmates who usually join our regular weekend study group.

**Leo:** Do you think Sam might feel overwhelmed if too many people show up at the restaurant?

**Maya:** No, it is no big deal for him because he truly enjoys meeting and talking with old friends.

**Leo:** Perfect. Let's keep the atmosphere chill and spend the whole afternoon catching up on recent news.

**Maya:** Sounds like a great plan! I will reserve a comfortable table by the window right away.
